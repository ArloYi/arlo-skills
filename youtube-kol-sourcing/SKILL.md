# YouTube KOL Sourcing

## Build the campaign profile first

Do not assume the audience, market, product category, creator niche, platform angle, excluded region, or partnership message.

Collect or infer these fields from the user's brief. Ask concise questions only when missing information would materially change sourcing:

- Product, brand, or campaign.
- Product outcome and customer job.
- Ideal audience.
- Target countries, regions, and languages.
- Creator categories or niches.
- Competitors, official channels, people, markets, or creator types to exclude.
- Desired pool size, strictness, budget tier, and any user-authorized fallback criteria.
- Required contact route, comment-review need, and prior outreach or cooperation lists.

Derive search keywords, fit criteria, exclusions, partnership angles, and output notes from this profile.

## Operating defaults

- Source YouTube first unless the user requests another platform.
- Prioritize precise small and mid-sized creators over large generic accounts. Keep headliner benchmarks separate.
- Never modify the user's source sheet, CRM, or database unless explicitly asked. Read prior lists only for deduplication.
- Treat explicit competitor people or reference sheets as exclusions. Do not treat search-keyword sheets as blacklists unless the user says they are.
- Do not lower quality gates to meet a quota. Put user-authorized fallback candidates in a separate conditional pool and label the exact condition.
- Prefer a visible creator persona. Require stronger fit and evidence for faceless channels.
- Final recommendation rows must be contact-ready. Put missing-contact candidates in review or watchlist only when useful.
- Treat comment review as a second-stage audience-quality check.
- Expand high-intent search batches before reporting an honest shortfall.

## Workflow

1. Complete the campaign profile.
2. Translate it into fit criteria, search themes, exclusions, and partnership angles.
3. Build candidate batches from direct and adjacent high-intent categories.
4. Verify channel identity, creator-led nature, region, competitor risk, recency, and recent relevant playback.
5. Deduplicate against prior lists and explicit competitor references.
6. Check contact readiness after the main filters pass.
7. Assign one best partnership angle and explain why the audience should care.
8. Route strict creators into `Small/Mid Creator Recommendations` or `Headliner Benchmark Creators`.
9. Route uncertain but promising candidates into `Active Review` or `Watchlist` only when useful.
10. Put user-authorized fallback rows in `Conditional Review Pool` with exact conditions and evidence.
11. Add `Dedupe Audit` only when deduplication is used; add comment-review fields only when comments are checked.
12. Add concise `Screening Notes` with campaign context, gates, exclusions, evidence limitations, and any shortfall.

## Strict recommendation gates

- Latest relevant upload within 30 days.
- Latest six relevant uploads average above 2,000 views.
- Latest six relevant uploads median at least 1,000 views.
- A usable public contact path.
- No ignored-market, competitor, official-channel, duplicate, or severe format-risk issue.
- No interview, podcast, or show-dominated channel unless the user explicitly permits that format.

Do not infer country, comments, contact information, views, sponsorship history, or ownership. Do not bypass login, CAPTCHA, or YouTube email-reveal restrictions. Examples from prior campaigns are not defaults for the current campaign.

## Final check

Confirm that strict final rows pass every gate; conditional rows are clearly separated; each row has one campaign-specific angle and audience-fit explanation; recent playback uses current evidence; contact fields name the exact public source or route; the `Owner` column is present; source sheets remain unchanged; and non-qualified creators are omitted or held outside strict pools.
