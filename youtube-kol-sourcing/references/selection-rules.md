# Selection rules

## Campaign-driven fit

Build creator-fit rules from the current campaign rather than reusing categories from prior work. Derive the priority creator types from the product, buyer, audience problem, desired content format, target geography and language, budget, and expected reach.

Downgrade channels that are generic, low effort, unrelated to the audience, repost-heavy, or recently off topic. Prefer creator-led channels with a visible persona or trusted expert voice. Faceless channels need stronger topic fit, recent playback, and contact evidence.

## Strict gates

A creator must pass every gate before entering either strict recommendation table:

- Relevant upload within the last 30 days.
- Latest six relevant uploads average above 2,000 views and median at least 1,000 views.
- At least half of recent relevant uploads match the campaign audience or use cases.
- Contact status is allowed for a final row.
- No ignored-market signal.
- No close competitor, competitor employee or founder, official product account, or strong competitor advocate.
- No interview, podcast, or show-dominated format unless explicitly allowed.

Prefer at least one year of visible history. Subscriber count and off-platform fame do not substitute for current YouTube evidence.

## Conditional acceptance

Use fallback criteria only when the user explicitly authorizes them. Keep ignored-market, competitor, duplicate, and contact-readiness gates. Separate these candidates in `Conditional Review Pool`, or add `Acceptance Condition`, `Condition Evidence`, and `Final Review Note`.

Name the exact exception: a user-defined playback floor, account-age exception, headliner-size exception, contact-form-only acceptance, company or agency risk, format risk, or another explicit condition. Never call a conditional row a strict recommendation.

## Exclusions

Apply user-defined market exclusions to country, city, language positioning, repeated content focus, and audience targeting. If geography is material but missing, ask; otherwise do not invent a blacklist.

Exclude close competitors, related people, and official channels. Verify identity through the handle, About page, ownership, and repeated product-owned content. Do not exclude a human creator merely because video titles mention tools.

Heavily downgrade interview or podcast channels, news publishers, official product-education channels, repost farms, and channels with recent off-topic drift. If the user permits one, label the risk.

## Contact readiness

Check contactability only after topic, recency, playback, region, competitor, and duplicate gates pass. Inspect the YouTube About page, channel and recent-video descriptions, linked sites and profile hubs, and agency or booking pages.

Use exactly one status:

- `Email verified`
- `YouTube email button present`
- `Website email found`
- `Agency / management contact`
- `Contact form only`
- `DM only`
- `No contact found`
- `Contact not checked`

Only the first five can enter final tables. If YouTube shows an email button but hides the address behind login, CAPTCHA, or a manual action, set `Email` to `Manual click required`. Never infer an address or accept a placeholder.

## Partnership angles

Choose exactly one best campaign-specific angle per final creator. Possible patterns include problem-to-solution story, workflow demo, before and after, comparison, expert education, use-case tutorial, community experiment, or an outcome built around trust, savings, revenue, convenience, status, or entertainment.

## Quality rules

- Do not add weak creators to meet a quota.
- High reach with weak fit belongs outside strict recommendations.
- No usable contact means no final recommendation.
- A prior-list match is not a new candidate.
- Report qualification shortfalls and the main rejection reasons honestly.
