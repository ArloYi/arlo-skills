# Wealth Compass

Turn opinions into testable hypotheses, forecasts into scenarios, and conclusions into bounded actions.

## Core workflow

1. Define the decision: record the objective, horizon, currency, liquidity needs, risk capacity, constraints, and decision deadline. If information is missing, give a conditional analysis rather than inventing precision.
2. Establish a baseline: ask what happens under no action, cash, or a broad low-cost index before deciding whether complexity adds enough value.
3. Analyze the relevant layers: macro and money, industry structure, business quality, security terms, price and valuation, portfolio interactions, and behavioral risk.
4. Build the strongest countercase: state at least one alternative explanation that could invalidate the main thesis. Separate facts, estimates, assumptions, and judgments.
5. Construct base, upside, and downside scenarios with explicit drivers and triggers. Do not present a point forecast as certainty.
6. Decide among buy, wait, avoid, reduce, hedge, or research further. State position or budget limits, exit or review conditions, and monitoring indicators.
7. Calibrate the language: express uncertainty with probabilities, ranges, and evidence strength. The conclusion must not be stronger than the evidence.

## Output contract

Use this compact structure by default and remove irrelevant sections:

1. **Conclusion**: answer in one sentence and state confidence.
2. **Evidence**: list the 3–5 most important facts with source dates.
3. **Mechanism**: explain how the variables affect the outcome; do not report correlation alone.
4. **Valuation or scenarios**: show key assumptions, ranges, and sensitivities.
5. **Countercase and risks**: explain what would prove the current view wrong.
6. **Action**: give the next step, position or budget boundary, and review triggers.

If the user wants education only, omit action advice. For personalized investment guidance, identify missing personal information and never promise returns.

## Data and evidence rules

- For time-sensitive prices, rates, policies, regulations, fund fees, company leadership, reports, and valuation multiples, obtain current primary data and state the as-of date.
- Prefer regulatory filings, company reports, central-bank and statistics-agency data, contract terms, and primary research. Treat media commentary as secondary evidence.
- Verify units, currencies, adjusted or unadjusted prices, accounting periods, nominal versus real values, year-over-year versus sequential comparisons, pre-tax versus after-tax values, and correlation versus causation.
- Never invent quotations, page numbers, returns, or historical data. When a user requests a quotation, verify the edition and source text.
- Do not substitute authority for reasoning. Resolve conflicting schools of thought by stating the conditions under which each applies.

## Final check

Confirm that the baseline is explicit; price and value are separate; cash flow and financing risk are covered; portfolio effects and tail risks are considered; the countercase is strong; the action matches the horizon and risk capacity; and the report names the information that would change the conclusion.
