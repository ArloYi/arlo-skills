# Portfolio, risk, and derivatives

## Start from the objective

Define the use of funds, horizon, minimum liquidity, maximum tolerable loss, liability currencies, taxes, and prohibited exposures. Risk capacity combines financial capacity, emotional capacity, and actual need; use the weakest of the three.

## Asset allocation

- Understand the portfolio through risk factors rather than asset labels: growth, inflation, duration, credit, liquidity, currency, volatility, and policy risk.
- Correlations can change during crises. Check shared funding sources, crowded trades, and forced-selling mechanisms.
- Start with a simple, low-cost, executable allocation, then prove why a complex strategy is necessary.
- Include fees, taxes, slippage, spreads, tracking error, and rebalancing friction in expected returns.

## Position sizing and rebalancing

Size positions according to edge, confidence, liquidity, tail loss, correlation, and reversibility. High confidence does not justify unlimited size. Define concentration limits by asset, theme, issuer, industry, country, currency, and liquidity.

Use calendar, threshold, or risk-budget rebalancing and state the trigger in advance. Do not redesign the rule at an emotional extreme.

## Measuring risk

Use maximum drawdown and recovery time, scenario stress, cash-flow shortfall, leverage and margin, liquidity days, duration and convexity, credit loss, concentration, and tail exposure. VaR is only a summary under a chosen distribution and horizon; it does not replace extreme scenarios.

## Options and futures

Identify contract multiplier, expiry, exercise, settlement, margin, basis, and liquidity before discussing direction.

- Futures returns combine spot changes, basis or roll, and collateral yield; leverage magnifies path and liquidity risk.
- Options require the underlying, strike, expiry, implied volatility, time value, and Delta, Gamma, Vega, and Theta exposures.
- Hedging must define the risk, protection range, cost, basis risk, and failure scenario. Protection does not eliminate risk.
- Treat option selling as underwriting income and stress-test gaps, volatility spikes, liquidity loss, and margin calls.

Do not give precise profit, loss, or margin conclusions without contract details. Distinguish expiry payoff from pre-expiry risk.

## Premortem

Assume the portfolio fails within a year. List the five most likely causes, such as thesis error, multiple compression, financing failure, correlation spikes, liquidity needs, or operational, custody, and regulatory events. Define warning indicators and pre-emptive actions for each.
