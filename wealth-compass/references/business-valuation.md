# Business, financial statements, and valuation

## From business to security

Answer in order: why customers pay; why revenue can persist; how much capital growth consumes; who ultimately receives the cash; and what expectations the current security price implies. A good business, a good security, and a good price are separate judgments.

## Business quality

- Business model: customers, value proposition, revenue model, cost structure, critical resources, and constraints.
- Moat: switching costs, network effects, cost advantage, brand or intangible assets, scale, and licenses. Validate with retention, pricing power, unit economics, or share evidence.
- Management: capital-allocation record, incentives, related-party transactions, dilution, promise delivery, and behavior under stress.
- Reinvestment: distinguish maintenance from growth investment, then estimate incremental returns and the runway for reinvestment.

## Financial-statement checks

Connect the income statement, balance sheet, and cash-flow statement: how profit becomes cash, how growth uses working capital, and how assets are financed.

Check revenue recognition and receivables, inventory accumulation, capitalized expenses, one-offs, goodwill and impairments, off-balance-sheet obligations, pensions and leases, stock compensation, buybacks alongside dilution, flattering free-cash-flow definitions, audit opinions, and accounting-policy changes.

At minimum, calculate or discuss revenue growth, gross and operating margins, cash conversion, return on invested capital, leverage and debt service, and per-share changes. Define industry-specific metrics.

## Choosing a valuation method

- Stable, predictable cash flow: DCF or owner earnings.
- Financial institutions: book value, capital adequacy, asset quality, net interest margin, and distributable earnings.
- Cyclical businesses: mid-cycle earnings and balance-sheet stress; never extrapolate peak profits.
- Growth companies: unit economics, penetration, margin path, and dilution across scenarios.
- Income assets: distribution coverage, asset life, refinancing, inflation pass-through, and tax.
- Relative valuation: compare only businesses with similar economics, growth, capital intensity, and accounting.

## Reverse valuation

Ask what the price requires. Backsolve the revenue growth, margins, capital efficiency, terminal value, or exit multiple implied by the market, then test whether the assumptions are mutually consistent. Use ranges and sensitivities rather than false decimal precision.

## Dividends and buybacks

Dividends can provide discipline and cash evidence, but a high yield may reflect a falling price or an unsustainable distribution. Check free-cash-flow coverage, leverage, capital expenditure, cyclicality, taxes, and growth opportunities. Buybacks create value only below intrinsic value, without damaging resilience, and when they reduce per-share dilution.

## Contrarian views and margin of safety

Being different from consensus is not an edge; being right and not yet priced is. State the consensus, the disagreement, evidence, catalyst, and why validation may take longer than expected. Build margin of safety through conservative assumptions, price discount, balance-sheet resilience, and position control.
