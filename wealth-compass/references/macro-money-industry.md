# Macro, money, and industry

## Causal chain

Use the chain `institutions and resources → productivity and supply → income and demand → credit and money → prices and rates → asset cash flows and discount rates`. Explain the mechanism, lag, and possible feedback at every arrow.

## Economy and wealth

- Wealth comes from sustainable productive capacity, institutions, skills, capital, and cooperation; it is not the same as the quantity of money.
- Distinguish stocks from flows, nominal from real, and aggregate from per-capita values. GDP growth does not automatically imply household welfare or investment returns.
- Compare choices through opportunity cost and marginal analysis. Exclude sunk costs from forward decisions.
- Prices aggregate distributed information, while externalities, public goods, monopoly, information asymmetry, and agency problems can distort outcomes.

## Money, banks, and central banks

- Money serves as a unit of account, medium of exchange, and store of value. Purchasing power reflects money, credit, productive capacity, velocity, and confidence together.
- Commercial-bank lending creates deposits. Constraints include capital, liquidity, collateral quality, regulation, funding costs, and borrower demand.
- Central banks influence financial conditions through policy rates, balance sheets, reserves, and expectations; transmission is neither mechanical nor immediate.
- Examine real rates, yield curves, credit spreads, credit growth, monetary-fiscal interaction, exchange rates, and inflation expectations. Do not infer the cycle from one indicator.

## Government, development, regions, and cities

- Analyze taxes, spending, transfers, debt sustainability, regulation, and incentives together rather than looking only at stimulus size.
- Development gaps can reflect institutions, human capital, infrastructure, demographics, trade position, technology diffusion, and capital-allocation efficiency.
- Agglomeration improves matching, learning, and shared infrastructure while increasing housing, congestion, pollution, and inequality costs.
- Regional analysis should track net migration, industrial diversity, fiscal capacity, housing supply, transport access, and public services.

## Industry structure

Check market boundaries, concentration, entry barriers, scale economies, network effects, substitutes, supplier and customer power, capacity cycles, regulation, and technology migration. A fast-growing industry can deliver weak shareholder returns because of competition and capital intensity; a slow-growing industry can deliver strong returns through discipline and barriers.

## Cycle analysis

Separate economic, credit, profit, inventory, capital-expenditure, property, and market-sentiment cycles. Identify leading, coincident, and lagging indicators, including contradictory evidence. Use cycles as a risk backdrop, not as a precision timing device.

## Scenario template

For each scenario, state growth, inflation, policy rates, credit conditions, margins, valuation multiples, beneficiaries and losers, and observable triggers. Prioritize cross-shocks such as sticky inflation during slowing growth or a sudden liquidity contraction.
