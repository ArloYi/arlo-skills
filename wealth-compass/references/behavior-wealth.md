# Behavior, decisions, and long-term wealth

## Discipline under uncertainty

Luck, skill, and risk jointly shape results. Do not infer ability from one success, and do not treat a low-probability event as impossible after it occurs. Manage both visible, accumulating threats and low-probability, high-impact tail events.

Use base rates, reference classes, Bayesian updating, and calibrated language. Record forecasts and reasoning before outcomes, then review them to reduce hindsight bias.

## Bias checklist

Check anchoring, confirmation bias, loss aversion, disposition effect, herding, recency, availability, overconfidence, illusion of control, sunk cost, incentive-caused bias, and identity protection. Do not merely label the bias; explain how it changes evidence selection, probability, or action.

## Inversion

Ask how the decision would certainly fail, then remove fragilities. For major decisions, state the thesis, strongest countercase, key unknowns, reversibility, opportunity cost, second-order effects, and stop conditions. A principle is useful only when it maps to observable behavior.

## Market narratives

Distinguish a story that explains data from a story that selects convenient data. Examine incentives for propagation, crowding, financing conditions, and price feedback. Mispricing can persist, so contrarian views require survival capacity and patient catalysts.

## Money psychology and life design

- Define “enough” as a measurable life objective to prevent comparison from driving unlimited risk.
- Saving rate, earning ability, investment return, time, and avoiding ruin jointly determine compounding. Early in the process, saving and skill are often more controllable than return.
- Preserve cash and time buffers for their option value and ability to prevent forced transactions.
- Separate one-time wealth, durable cash flow, and long-term capital appreciation. Measure the capital, maintenance labor, risk, tax, and durability behind any “passive income.”
- Do not equate asset ownership with debt financing. Evaluate an asset by net cash flow, risk, control, liquidity, and opportunity cost.

## Decision journal

Record the date, question, choices, baseline, known facts, key assumptions, probabilities, expected gains and losses, strongest countercase, action and size, invalidation conditions, and review date. During review, separate process quality from outcome quality.
