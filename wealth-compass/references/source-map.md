# Source-theme map

This file maps the original reading list behind the framework to its thematic contributions. It is not a set of book summaries, a substitute for reading, or a quotation source. Verify editions, authors, translated titles, and original text before citing.

## Investing, valuation, and business

- *The Intelligent Investor* and *Security Analysis*: intrinsic value, margin of safety, market volatility, and the distinction between investment and speculation.
- *Berkshire Hathaway Letters to Shareholders*, *The Snowball*, and *Poor Charlie's Almanack*: business quality, capital allocation, compounding, circle of competence, incentives, and multidisciplinary thinking.
- *Dividends Don't Lie* and Templeton's contrarian investing: dividend evidence, contrarian discipline, and valuation.
- Financial-statement narrative texts: linking the three statements, business narrative, and accounting-quality checks.
- *Where Are the Customers' Yachts?*: intermediary incentives, fees, and conflicts of interest.

## Markets, risk, and portfolios

- *A Random Walk Down Wall Street*: market efficiency, diversification, cost, and passive-investing baselines.
- *Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives*: contract mechanics, Greeks, hedging, and leverage risk.
- Cycle frameworks: economic, credit, profit, psychological, and market cycles.
- *The Black Swan*, *Fooled by Randomness*, and gray-rhino frameworks: randomness, tail risk, survivorship bias, and visible unresolved threats.
- *The Alchemy of Finance*: reflexivity, feedback between beliefs and prices, and narrative mechanisms.

## Economics, money, and institutions

- General economics, *The Wealth of Nations*, and island-economy explanations: specialization, exchange, incentives, opportunity cost, prices, and production.
- Central banking, money and banking, and purchasing-power texts: money, credit, banks, and monetary transmission.
- Monetary and financial history: institutional evolution and recurring crisis patterns.
- Industrial and public economics: industry organization, market failure, public choice, and policy tools.
- Development, regional, and urban economics: growth, institutions, agglomeration, regions, and cities.

## Behavior and wealth systems

- *The Psychology of Money*: wealth behavior, saving, tail outcomes, freedom, and enough.
- *Principles*: reviewable decision systems, explicit rules, and learning from error.
- *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant*: specific knowledge, leverage, long horizons, and life choices.
- Popular cash-flow and financial-independence texts: asset/liability awareness and income systems, supplemented with capital requirements, maintenance costs, risk, and survivorship-bias checks.

## Resolving conflicts

Translate conflicting claims into conditions. The more efficient the market, the higher the costs, and the harder an edge is to verify, the stronger the case for low-cost diversification. Active and concentrated strategies become defensible only when an investor can demonstrate a sustainable edge after costs. Use macro views for scenarios and risk budgets, not as a substitute for security valuation. Use derivatives to define exposures or hedges, not to disguise leverage as a source of return.
