# Source map

Use this as a search starting point, not a claim of exhaustive monitoring or a fixed list of sites successfully checked. Locate current official pages at execution time; paths, product names, and availability can change. Follow an unexpected consequential source even if it is absent here.

## Official releases and products

Start with official blogs, newsrooms, research pages, developer documentation, changelogs, release notes, repositories, and attributable official posts from:

OpenAI; Anthropic; Google and Google DeepMind; Meta AI; Microsoft; GitHub; NVIDIA; xAI; Mistral AI; Hugging Face; Amazon/AWS; Apple; Adobe; Runway; Midjourney; ElevenLabs; Perplexity; Cursor; Replit; Cognition/Devin; and Cloudflare.

Track substantive releases from other labs, companies, and open-source teams as they emerge. Check product access and documentation, not only launch announcements. Distinguish preview, waitlist, limited access, general availability, API access, and downloadable weights.

## Developer ecosystem

Inspect relevant GitHub releases, important issues and pull requests, security advisories, trending projects, and Hacker News or comparable technical discussions. Check what is actually implemented and released: an open PR is not a shipped feature.

Prioritize agents, AI coding, MCP, skills, agent harnesses, browser/desktop agents, computer use, local AI, inference serving, memory, RAG, tool use, multi-agent systems, and agent infrastructure.

For a project gaining attention, identify the practical reason: a new workflow, capability, integration, reliability improvement, or failure mode. Stars and discussion counts support discovery but do not establish quality. A star-growth claim needs two dated observations or a reliable dated series; a current total alone cannot prove growth.

## Research

Search arXiv and official research publications from major AI labs and universities, including Stanford, MIT, Berkeley, CMU, Princeton, and Oxford, as relevant.

Look for consequential findings in reasoning, agents, long context, memory, multi-agent behavior, safety, interpretability, AI for science, multimodality, training, inference, test-time compute, and model efficiency.

Inspect the paper's actual experiments, evaluation setup, limitations, and publication/version dates. Distinguish preprints from reviewed publications, author claims from independent replication, and benchmark performance from demonstrated real-world capability. Favor findings that alter a capability boundary or challenge an existing assumption over routine ranking changes.

## Independent checks

Look for independent benchmarks such as Artificial Analysis, reproducible third-party evaluations, developer tests, and community replications. Compare model versions, task definitions, hardware, prices, sampling settings, and baselines where they affect a claim. Label a single anecdotal test as such.

## Media and community leads

Use Reuters, TechCrunch, The Verge, Ars Technica, WIRED, Financial Times, Bloomberg, 404 Media, MIT Technology Review, Semafor, and other reliable outlets to discover investigations, regulation, copyright disputes, infrastructure changes, and commercial developments.

Follow reporting back to company statements, regulatory filings, court documents, repositories, or research where possible. Quality exclusive reporting can stand on its own when no primary statement exists, but its attributed and unconfirmed nature must remain clear.

Access-limited pages, unavailable X posts, search snippets, and community summaries are leads. Do not describe their underlying contents as inspected. Seek an accessible original source or explicitly narrow the claim. Never infer exhaustive coverage from a successful search query.
